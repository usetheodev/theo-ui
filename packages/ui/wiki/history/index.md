Records kept because they explain **why the current state is the way it is**. Nothing here
is normative — where a history concept disagrees with a design-system or architecture
concept, the current one wins.

## Why keep any of it

Two of these are load-bearing for decisions that are still in force. The design-direction
record is what a `## Histórico` gate section points at, and it is the reason someone
encountering "Boska" in git history knows it was abandoned rather than pending. The
competitor audit is what makes "no violet-magenta gradients" a reasoned constraint rather
than an arbitrary taste.

The rest is kept because a release record that omits its own divergences is not a record.
